3

What actually happens on launch

100% of the token supply goes straight into a Uniswap liquidity pool — there's no vault, no team allocation, nothing held back.

The LP position (an NFT that represents that liquidity) is sent to a dedicated Custodian contract for that token. Right after, ownership of both the token and the Custodian is renounced — permanently. No one, including the creator, can ever withdraw that liquidity again.

💡 To be precise: the Custodian doesn't hold ETH or "swap" anything itself. It simply holds the LP position NFT and has no withdrawal function. All actual buying and selling happens through the Uniswap pool directly — the Custodian's only job is to make sure the liquidity can never be pulled out.
4

Why liquidity looks small right after launch

Every new token starts with a small warm-up amount of ETH paired against the full token supply — so liquidity (and market cap) shown right after launch will look low. That's expected, not a red flag.

Liquidity grows naturally as real buyers add ETH to the pool through trading. A low starting number just means the token is brand new.

Claiming your fees

If your token gets trading activity, fees build up automatically — here's how to claim them, whether it's your own token or someone else's (claiming is permissionless, the 50/50 split is locked in forever).

1. Open the Factory, connect your wallet, and scroll down to "Local launch history". Find your token and click "Proof page".

2. On the proof page, scroll to the "Claim your fees" section. It gives you two direct links straight to the right contract on the block explorer — no searching needed.

3. Click "Open Custodian → collect()", connect your wallet on the explorer, and hit Write. This moves the accumulated fees from the liquidity position into the FeeSplitter.

4. Click "Open FeeSplitter → release()". Fees usually come in as two assets — the token itself and WETH — so the proof page shows two addresses to copy, one at a time. Paste the first into the token field, connect your wallet, hit Write — then repeat with the second address.

💡 collect() takes no input — just connect and click Write. release() needs an address pasted in manually, twice per token (once for the token, once for WETH) — the proof page always shows both, ready to copy.
