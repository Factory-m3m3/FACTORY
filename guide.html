3

What actually happens on launch

100% of the token supply goes straight into a Uniswap liquidity pool — there's no vault, no team allocation, nothing held back.

The LP position (an NFT that represents that liquidity) is sent to a dedicated Custodian contract for that token. Right after, ownership of both the token and the Custodian is renounced — permanently. No one, including the creator, can ever withdraw that liquidity again.

💡 To be precise: the Custodian doesn't hold ETH or "swap" anything itself. It simply holds the LP position NFT and has no withdrawal function. All actual buying and selling happens through the Uniswap pool directly — the Custodian's only job is to make sure the liquidity can never be pulled out.
4

Why liquidity looks small right after launch

Every new token starts with a small warm-up amount of ETH paired against the full token supply — so liquidity (and market cap) shown right after launch will look low. That's expected, not a red flag.

Liquidity grows naturally as real buyers add ETH to the pool through trading. A low starting number just means the token is brand new.

Claiming your fees

If a token gets trading activity, fees build up automatically — here's how to claim them, whether it's your own token or someone else's (claiming is permissionless, the 50/50 split is locked in forever).

1. Open the Catalog and click the token you want to claim fees for. Every token launched through the Factory gets its own public proof page there.

Open the Catalog →

2. On that proof page, note down two addresses from the table: "Adresse du Custodian" and "Fee recipient" (this second one is actually the token's FeeSplitter contract, despite the label).

3. Paste the Custodian address into the block explorer's search bar, open the Write contract tab, connect your wallet, and call collect() — no input needed. This moves the accumulated fees from the liquidity position into the FeeSplitter.

Open the block explorer →

4. Now paste the FeeSplitter address (the "Fee recipient" one) into the block explorer, same Write contract tab, and call release(address token) — twice. Fees always come in as two assets: the memecoin itself, and WETH.

→ First run: paste the token's own contract address (visible at the top of the same proof page).
→ Second run: paste the WETH address below — it's fixed, the same on every single token, so you never need to look it up again.
